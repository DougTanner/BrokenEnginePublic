Water Foam/Whitecaps and Refraction
=====================================

Context
-------
The water surface currently renders Gerstner waves at two frequency bands
(low and medium) in Water.vert, with depth-based LUT coloring, animated
normal maps, Fresnel skybox reflections, specular lighting, and shadow
mapping in Water.frag. There is no foam/whitecap rendering and no
refraction of the scene beneath the water surface. The water is drawn
with alpha blending into the swapchain framebuffer after opaque models
and terrain, inside RecordMainCommandBuffer (CommandBufferManager.cpp
line 705).

Foam appears naturally at wave crests where steepness is high (the
Jacobian of the Gerstner displacement approaches zero, indicating wave
breaking). Refraction requires sampling the scene rendered so far at
offset UV coordinates, which means copying the framebuffer color before
the water draw.

Why
---
- Wave crests lack visual definition; foam/whitecaps add surface detail
  that sells the ocean at all distances
- Shallow water near shores should accumulate foam where waves compress,
  matching real-world surf behavior
- Refraction of the scene beneath the surface (terrain, submerged
  objects) adds depth and realism, especially in shallow clear water
- Both features use data already available in the vertex shader (wave
  steepness, terrain elevation) with minimal new uniform data

2. Engine/Data/Shaders/Water/Water.vert
   Output the partial derivatives of the Gerstner displacement to
   compute a Jacobian-based foam factor per vertex.

   a) Add a new output after f3OutNormal (location 3):
        layout (location = 4) out float fOutFoamFactor;

   b) In the Gerstner function, accumulate the Jacobian of the
      horizontal displacement. For each wave i in both low and medium
      loops, track the partial derivatives of the XY displacement with
      respect to the original XY position. The Jacobian determinant of
      the summed Gerstner displacement measures how much the surface
      area is compressed; values near zero indicate wave breaking.

      After the low wave loop, add two float accumulators (dPdx_x,
      dPdy_y, initialized to 1.0) and accumulate:
        dPdx_x -= steepness * amplitude * direction.x * direction.x * omega * sin(radians)
        dPdy_y -= steepness * amplitude * direction.y * direction.y * omega * sin(radians)
      (Apply the same accumulation in the medium wave loop.)

      After both loops, compute:
        float fJacobian = dPdx_x * dPdy_y;
        fOutFoamFactor = clamp(1.0 - fJacobian / globalLayout.fWaterFoamStart, 0.0, 1.0);

      Add shore foam by blending with terrain elevation:
        float fShoreFoam = clamp(-fTerrainElevation / globalLayout.fWaterFoamShoreDepth, 0.0, 1.0);
        fShoreFoam = 1.0 - fShoreFoam;  // 1.0 at shore, 0.0 in deep water
        fOutFoamFactor = max(fOutFoamFactor, fShoreFoam);

3. Engine/Data/Shaders/Water/Water.frag
   a) Add a new input matching the vertex output:

   b) Add the scene color sampler for refraction (binding 12, after
      smokeSampler at binding 11) and a foam texture sampler (binding

        layout (set = 1, binding = 13) uniform sampler2D foamTextureSampler;

   c) Add refraction sampling early in main(), after the terrain
      elevation early-out and before color computation.

      Convert the fragment's clip-space position to screen UV:
        vec2 f2ScreenUV = gl_FragCoord.xy / vec2(textureSize(sceneColorSampler, 0));

      Compute a refraction offset from the sampled normal, scaled by
      depth (shallow water refracts more visibly) and the uniform:
        vec2 f2RefractionOffset = globalLayout.fWaterRefractionStrength
            * f3SampledNormal.xy
            * clamp(-fTerrainElevation * 0.5, 0.0, 1.0);
        vec3 f3RefractionColor = texture(sceneColorSampler, f2ScreenUV + f2RefractionOffset).xyz;

      (The sampled normal calculation already exists at lines 66-72;
      move the refraction sampling to just after it, before color
      mixing.)

   d) Blend refraction into the pre-lighting color. Replace the current
      depth color / water color mix with a three-way blend:
      - Deep water: water color (existing behavior)
      - Shallow water: refraction color (scene beneath surface)
      - Blend factor: terrain depth

      After computing f3PreLightingColor (line 82), blend with
      refraction:
        float fRefractionBlend = clamp(fHeight * globalLayout.fWaterDepthColorFeather, 0.0, 1.0);
        f3PreLightingColor = mix(f3RefractionColor, f3PreLightingColor, fRefractionBlend);

   e) Add foam compositing after shadow computation and before the
      final f4OutColor write (after line 113). Sample the foam texture
      with animated UVs, modulate by fInFoamFactor, and additively
      blend as a bright surface layer:

        if (globalLayout.iWaterHasFoamTexture > 0)
        {
            float fFoamTime = globalLayout.fElapsedTime * globalLayout.fWaterFoamSpeed;
            vec2 f2FoamUV = f3InPosition.xy * globalLayout.fWaterFoamNoiseScale;
            float fFoamNoise = texture(foamTextureSampler, f2FoamUV + vec2(fFoamTime, 0.0)).x;
            fFoamNoise *= texture(foamTextureSampler, 1.3 * f2FoamUV + vec2(0.0, -0.7 * fFoamTime)).x;
            float fFoam = fInFoamFactor * globalLayout.fWaterFoamScale * fFoamNoise;
            // Foam is bright white, modulated by sunlight
            f4OutColor.xyz = mix(f4OutColor.xyz, fSunlight * vec3(1.0), clamp(fFoam, 0.0, 1.0));
        }

      Foam darkening for wave troughs (ambient occlusion approximation):
        f4OutColor.xyz *= max(1.0 + f3InPosition.z * 0.5, 0.5);

      This line goes just before the foam block, darkening areas below
      z=0 to simulate self-shadowing in wave troughs.

6. Engine/Source/Graphics/Managers/CommandBufferManager.cpp
   Insert a framebuffer-to-texture copy between the terrain draw and
   the water draw in RecordMainCommandBuffer.

   After the terrain RecordDraw (line 702) and before the water
   RecordDraw (line 705):

   a) End the current render pass:
        Texture::RecordEndRenderPass(vkCommandBuffer);

   b) Resolve the multisampled framebuffer color to the scene color
      copy texture using vkCmdResolveImage (if MSAA) or vkCmdCopyImage
      (if no MSAA). Transition the scene color copy texture from
      kShaderReadOnly to kTransferDestination, perform the copy from
      the swapchain present image (or multisampled resolve), then
      transition back to kShaderReadOnly.

      The swapchain present image must be transitioned from
      COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL to TRANSFER_SRC, copied, then
      transitioned back to COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL.

   c) Re-begin the render pass with VK_ATTACHMENT_LOAD_OP_LOAD (not
      CLEAR) to preserve the already-rendered opaque geometry.

   The render pass must be split at this point because Vulkan does not
   allow sampling an attachment that is currently being written to
   within the same render pass.

Notes
-----
- The render pass split in step 6 is the most complex part. An
  alternative is to copy the multisampled resolve target after the
  render pass ends and draw water in a second render pass, but that
  would require a second render pass with depth load, which is
  equivalent complexity. The mid-pass split is preferred because it
  keeps water in the same depth buffer context as terrain.
- If HdrResolveAndColorGrading is implemented first, the scene color
  copy should sample the HDR intermediate texture instead of the
  swapchain framebuffer, and the format should match
  (R16G16B16A16_SFLOAT).
- The foam texture should be a tileable grayscale noise/cell pattern
  (BC4 compressed). A Voronoi/cellular noise texture works well for
  the organic look of sea foam.
- Shore foam (step 2) uses terrain elevation as a proxy for wave
  compression near coastlines. This is simple but effective; a more
  physically accurate approach would track wave energy convergence,
  which is unnecessary for visual quality.
- The Jacobian-based foam in step 2 automatically concentrates foam at
  wave crests where the surface is most compressed, matching the
  reference paper "Ocean Wave Rendering with Whitecap in the Visual
  System of a Maritime Simulator."
- Wave trough darkening (step 3e) is a cheap approximation of ambient
  occlusion within wave valleys, replacing more expensive ray-based AO.
- The refraction UV offset is clamped by terrain depth so that deep
  water does not show exaggerated distortion, and the normal-based
  offset naturally varies with wave animation.
