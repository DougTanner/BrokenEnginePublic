Context
-------
Snow detection in Terrain.frag (lines 62-63) is purely color-based:
it measures distance from the terrain color to white, scaled by
fTerrainSnowMultiplier.  This means snow covers flat plateaus and
concave grooves equally.  In reality, snow accumulates preferentially
in concave areas (erosion grooves, crevices) and gets blown off convex
ridges.  The ambient occlusion texture (binding 8) and normal map
(binding 7) are already sampled in Terrain.frag but are not used to
influence snow distribution.

Snow follows hydrolic erosion grooves, as seen on Mt. Fuji where snow
fills the radial gullies carved into the volcanic cone while exposed
ridges between grooves remain bare.

Why
---
- Concave terrain features (grooves, gullies) should trap more snow,
  while convex ridges should shed it -- matching real-world erosion
  patterns like Mt. Fuji
- The AO texture already encodes surface concavity (low AO = concave,
  occluded areas) and is sampled but only used for final lighting, not
  snow distribution
- Adding curvature awareness to snow requires only shader math changes
  and two new tunable uniforms -- no new textures or render passes

Changes (4 files)
-----------------

1. Engine/Data/Shaders/Terrain/Terrain.frag
   After line 63 (the existing fSnowPercent calculation), boost snow in
   concave areas using the AO texture as a concavity proxy.  Low AO
   values indicate concave/occluded geometry (grooves), high AO values
   indicate convex/exposed geometry (ridges).  Replace lines 62-63
   with:

     vec3 f3SnowDiff = abs(f3Color - vec3(1.0f, 1.0f, 1.0f));
     float fSnowPercent = 1.0f - clamp(globalLayout.fTerrainSnowMultiplier * (f3SnowDiff.x + f3SnowDiff.y + f3SnowDiff.z), 0.0f, 1.0f);

     // Curvature-aware snow: AO encodes concavity (low AO = grooves)
     float fAO = texture(ambientOcclusionTextureSampler, f2InVisibleAreaTexcoord).x;
     float fConcavity = 1.0f - fAO;
     float fSnowCurvatureBias = mix(-globalLayout.fTerrainSnowCurvature, globalLayout.fTerrainSnowCurvature, fConcavity);
     fSnowPercent = clamp(fSnowPercent + fSnowCurvatureBias, 0.0f, 1.0f);

   This shifts fSnowPercent up in concave areas (grooves get more snow)
   and down on convex ridges (exposed areas lose snow).  The AO sample
   is reused from the existing line 95 read -- the texture cache will
   serve it at near-zero cost.

   Note: the existing AO sample on line 95 remains unchanged for
   lighting.  This new sample is a separate read at the same texcoord
   for the snow calculation, which occurs before the lighting section.

Notes
-----
- The AO texture is a natural concavity proxy: areas with low ambient
  occlusion are geometrically concave (surrounded by nearby surfaces
  that block light), which correlates with erosion grooves
- fTerrainSnowCurvature at 0.0 disables the effect entirely (pure
  color-based snow as before); at 1.0 the full AO-derived bias applies
- The mix from -curvature to +curvature means convex ridges (high AO,
  fConcavity near 0) get snow reduced and concave grooves (low AO,
  fConcavity near 1) get snow added, with a neutral midpoint at
  AO = 0.5
- No new texture bindings or render passes are needed -- this reuses
  the existing AO sampler already bound at set 1, binding 8
- The two repurposed padding fields maintain std140 alignment since
  they occupy the same float slots
